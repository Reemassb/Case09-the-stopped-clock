EVIDENCE & EQUATIONS  ·  CASE #09

The Stopped Clock

Simple harmonic motion · pendulum timekeeping · reconstructing a time of impact from a clock that was not keeping time.

CASE BRIEFING

A display cabinet in the strongroom of Harrow & Vance, horologists, was forced after hours. A longcase clock stood in front of it, in for service and waiting to be collected, and whoever opened the cabinet shoved the clock aside to reach it. A pendulum stops at a far smaller jolt than that. The movement stopped, and the dial has not been touched since.

That dial is the only clock in the room. Investigators are using its stopped reading to establish whether the clock was struck while the workshop technician, D. Vance, was badged into the building.

There is a complication. The clock came in for service and its pendulum was rehung at the wrong effective length. It was set correct against the speaking clock when the work was signed off, and nobody has looked at it since — it is a customer’s piece, shelved and waiting. It has been gaining or losing ever since.

The trap, before you start. The dial tells you how many oscillations the clock counted, not how long they took. Those are the same thing only if the pendulum is at its design length, and yours is not.
